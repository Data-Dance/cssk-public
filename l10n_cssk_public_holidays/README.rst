==============================================
Public Holidays: Slovakia and Czech Republic
==============================================

.. image:: https://img.shields.io/badge/licence-AGPL--3-blue.png
   :alt: License: AGPL-3

**Generates the Slovak and Czech days of rest into the OCA public holiday calendar, year by year, with the statutory exceptions.**

What it is for
================

Leave counting and attendance must not count a day of rest as a working day. The OCA module ``calendar_public_holiday`` (used by ``hr_holidays_public`` and the OCA attendance and payroll modules) keeps one public holiday record per year and country, but leaves it empty. This module fills it with the Slovak and Czech **days of rest** (*dni pracovného pokoja* / *dny pracovního klidu*).

Only days of rest are generated. A holiday that the law makes a working day in a given year is left out of that year, because leave and attendance must count it as one. Payroll holiday pay follows its own rules and is not affected.

Legal background
==================

**Slovakia** — `§ 1 and § 2 of Act No. 241/1993 <https://www.slov-lex.sk/ezbierky/pravne-predpisy/SK/ZZ/1993/241/#paragraf-2>`__: 1 January, 6 January, Good Friday, Easter Monday, 1 May, 8 May, 5 July, 29 August, 15 September, 1 November and 24–26 December. The exceptions the module applies:

- 1 September (*Deň Ústavy*) is a day of rest up to 2023 only, and 17 November up to 2024 only. `§ 2 ods. 3 <https://www.slov-lex.sk/ezbierky/pravne-predpisy/SK/ZZ/1993/241/#paragraf-2.odsek-3>`__ keeps them (and 28 October) as state holidays that are not days of rest.

- 8 May and 15 September are working days **in 2026 only**: the transitional `§ 4b <https://www.slov-lex.sk/ezbierky/pravne-predpisy/SK/ZZ/1993/241/#paragraf-4b>`__ names that year and no other, so both return in 2027 unless the law changes again.

**Czech Republic** — `§ 1 to § 3 of Act No. 245/2000 Coll. <https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2000/245>`__: 1 January, Good Friday (since 2016), Easter Monday, 1 May, 8 May, 5 July, 6 July, 28 September, 28 October, 17 November and 24–26 December.

How it runs
=============

- At installation the current and the next year are generated for both countries (if the country exists in the database).

- A monthly scheduled action, *Public holidays: generate next year (SK, CZ)*, keeps the current and the next year present, so a new year appears on its own.

- The records are under *Calendar → Configuration → Public Holidays*.

Generation only adds what is missing. Lines already present for a date are kept, including your edits; a regional line (one with states) does not count as the nationwide day. To force a change, delete the line or the whole year and run the scheduled action again (*Settings → Technical → Scheduled Actions*, *Run Manually*).

Limitations
=============

- The rules are a table in the code. When the law changes, the module must be updated; years already generated are not corrected automatically — delete the affected year and regenerate it.

- Only Slovakia and the Czech Republic, nationwide days only.

- Sundays are not generated: the calendar lists holidays, and working time is defined by the employee's working schedule.
